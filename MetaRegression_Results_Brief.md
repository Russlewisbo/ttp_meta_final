# Meta-Regression Analysis: Brief Results Section

## Meta-Regression Analysis (Brief Version)

### For manuscripts with strict word limits (~400-500 words)

---

To explore sources of the substantial between-study heterogeneity (I² = 98.6%), we conducted univariable meta-regression analyses examining four potential moderators: pathogen class (Gram-positive, Gram-negative, or mixed), overall risk of bias (low/moderate vs high/critical), publication year, and TTP cutpoint (in hours). All models were fitted using restricted maximum likelihood estimation.

**TTP cutpoint was the only significant moderator** (QM = 11.01, p < 0.001), with shorter cutpoints associated with larger effect sizes (coefficient = -0.064 log-OR per hour, 95% CI: -0.10 to -0.03). This represents approximately a 6% reduction in the pooled odds ratio for each additional hour of the cutpoint threshold. Studies using cutpoints below 12 hours (k = 13) reported odds ratios ranging from 2.5 to 8.0, whereas studies using cutpoints ≥24 hours (k = 5) reported more modest odds ratios between 1.0 and 2.5. Importantly, TTP cutpoint explained 27.3% of between-study heterogeneity, reducing residual I² from 98.6% to 71.3% (τ from 0.68 to 0.51).

**Other moderators did not significantly explain heterogeneity.** Pathogen class showed no significant moderator effect (QM = 3.97, p = 0.137; residual I² = 98.3%), with similar effect sizes observed across Gram-positive (k = 12), Gram-negative (k = 14), and mixed populations (k = 7). This finding corroborates our stratified analysis demonstrating TTP as a pathogen-agnostic prognostic marker. Risk of bias showed a non-significant trend (QM = 2.74, p = 0.098), with high-risk studies (k = 2) showing numerically smaller effects than low/moderate-risk studies (k = 31), though limited power precluded definitive assessment. Publication year showed a borderline trend (QM = 3.22, p = 0.073) suggesting slightly larger effects in recent studies, but explained minimal heterogeneity (residual I² = 98.0%).

The significant effect of TTP cutpoint likely reflects differential risk stratification at different time thresholds. Shorter cutpoints (8-12 hours) more specifically identify patients with extremely high bacterial inocula at highest mortality risk, whereas longer cutpoints (24+ hours) capture heterogeneous populations with diluted prognostic signal. Many studies used data-driven cutpoint selection via ROC analysis, which may have contributed to heterogeneity by selecting different thresholds based on local characteristics.

The substantial residual heterogeneity (71.3%) indicates that unmeasured factors contribute to variability, including patient severity (APACHE, SOFA scores), antibiotic appropriateness and timing, source control adequacy, comorbidity burden, blood culture system differences, and statistical adjustment approaches. Future studies should employ standardized TTP cutpoints (e.g., 12 hours) and comprehensively report key confounders to facilitate evidence synthesis and enable individual patient data meta-analyses.

**In summary,** TTP cutpoint significantly moderated the TTP-mortality association, explaining 27% of heterogeneity, while other examined moderators did not. Findings support TTP as a pathogen-agnostic prognostic marker and highlight the need for standardized measurement and reporting practices.

## Ultra-Brief Version (150-200 words)

Meta-regression analyses examined four potential moderators of the TTP-mortality association: pathogen class, risk of bias, publication year, and TTP cutpoint. **Only TTP cutpoint significantly explained heterogeneity** (QM = 11.01, p < 0.001), with shorter cutpoints associated with larger effect sizes (coefficient = -0.064 log-OR per hour, 95% CI: -0.10 to -0.03). Studies using cutpoints <12 hours reported ORs of 2.5-8.0, whereas studies using ≥24-hour cutpoints reported ORs of 1.0-2.5. TTP cutpoint explained 27% of between-study heterogeneity, reducing residual I² from 98.6% to 71.3%.

Other moderators showed no significant effects: pathogen class (p = 0.137), risk of bias (p = 0.098), and publication year (p = 0.073). The lack of pathogen class moderation confirms TTP as a universal prognostic marker. Substantial residual heterogeneity (71%) likely reflects unmeasured confounders including disease severity, antibiotic timing, and source control adequacy. Findings highlight the need for standardized TTP cutpoints and comprehensive confounder reporting in future studies.
