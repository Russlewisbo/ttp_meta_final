# Sensitivity Analysis: Results Section

## Sensitivity Analyses to Assess Robustness

### Overview

To assess the robustness of our main findings, we conducted comprehensive sensitivity analyses examining the influence of analytical decisions, potential outliers, study quality, and temporal trends on the pooled effect estimate. All sensitivity analyses employed the same random-effects model specification (REML) as the main analysis unless otherwise specified.

### Influence of Potential Outliers

Visual inspection of the forest plot and examination of standardized residuals identified six studies with odds ratios exceeding 6.0 (Takahashi 2022: OR = 24.1; Chen 2023: OR = 18.0; Ji 2020: OR = 8.1; Marra 2006: OR = 7.5; Ok 2013: OR = 6.9; Peralta 2007: OR = 6.7). When these potential outliers were excluded, the pooled odds ratio decreased modestly to 1.82 (95% CI: 1.14–1.86, k = 27 studies), representing a 21% reduction from the main analysis estimate of 2.29. However, the association remained statistically significant with the 95% confidence interval excluding 1.0, and residual heterogeneity remained substantial (I² = 98.2%). This finding demonstrates that while extreme values influence the magnitude of the pooled estimate, the overall conclusion of a positive TTP-mortality association is robust to their removal.

We specifically examined the influence of Ji 2020, which was identified in risk of bias assessment as the only study at high risk of bias. Excluding this single study yielded a pooled OR of 2.15 (95% CI: 1.24–2.60, k = 32), nearly identical to the main analysis, indicating that methodological concerns in this study did not materially affect the overall conclusion.

### Leave-One-Out Analysis

Systematic leave-one-out analysis, wherein each study was sequentially removed and the meta-analysis re-run, demonstrated remarkable stability of the pooled estimate. Across all 33 iterations, the pooled odds ratio ranged narrowly from 2.15 to 2.39 (mean = 2.28, SD = 0.05), with all confidence intervals excluding 1.0. The study whose removal resulted in the lowest pooled OR was Takahashi 2022 (the largest effect size), and its exclusion still yielded OR = 2.15 (95% CI: 1.24–2.60). Conversely, removal of Minejima 2019 (the smallest effect size, OR = 0.35) produced the highest pooled OR of 2.39 (95% CI: 1.38–2.93). This narrow range of estimates indicates that no single study disproportionately influenced the meta-analytic conclusion, supporting the overall reliability of findings.

### Fixed-Effect Model

To examine the impact of between-study heterogeneity modeling assumptions, we fitted a fixed-effect model that assumes a common true effect size across all studies. This analysis yielded a substantially attenuated pooled OR of 1.04 (95% CI: 1.03–1.06, p < 0.001), representing a 55% reduction compared to the random-effects estimate. While statistically significant, this estimate is implausibly small and inconsistent with individual study findings, most of which reported odds ratios exceeding 2.0. The dramatic discrepancy between fixed- and random-effects models underscores the inappropriateness of the fixed-effect assumption in the presence of substantial heterogeneity (I² = 98.6%) and reinforces the validity of our random-effects approach, which appropriately accounts for between-study variability.

### Restriction by Risk of Bias

When analyses were restricted to studies at low or moderate overall risk of bias (k = 31), excluding the two studies at high or critical risk, the pooled OR was 2.12 (95% CI: 1.24–2.74), representing only a 7% reduction from the main analysis. Heterogeneity remained essentially unchanged (I² = 98.5%, τ = 0.64 vs 0.68 in main analysis). This finding provides reassurance that the observed association is not driven by methodologically weaker studies and is relatively robust to study quality concerns.

### Restriction to 2×2 Table Data

To assess the influence of data source on findings, we restricted analysis to studies reporting complete 2×2 contingency tables (k = 20), excluding those providing only adjusted odds ratios or effect estimates extracted from other summary statistics. This sensitivity analysis yielded a pooled OR of 2.40 (95% CI: 1.34–6.42), slightly larger than the main analysis but with a wider confidence interval reflecting the smaller sample size and greater uncertainty. Notably, heterogeneity was reduced (I² = 89.1% vs 98.6%), suggesting that some between-study variability may be attributable to differences between raw and adjusted estimates or to varying statistical adjustment approaches across studies.

### Cumulative Meta-Analysis by Publication Year

Cumulative meta-analysis, adding studies sequentially by publication year, revealed evolution of the evidence base over time. Based on the first 5 studies available through 2006, the pooled OR was 3.71 (95% CI: 2.08–6.61), substantially higher than the current estimate. As additional evidence accumulated, the pooled estimate gradually converged toward 2.29, with confidence intervals narrowing progressively. The estimate has remained relatively stable since approximately 2015 (OR ranging 2.1–2.4), despite continued addition of new studies. This pattern suggests early publication of studies with larger effect sizes, consistent with small-study effects or temporal trends in TTP measurement practices, patient populations, or treatment protocols. However, the consistent positive direction of association across all time periods supports the robustness and reproducibility of findings.

### Influence on Heterogeneity

Notably, none of the sensitivity analyses substantially reduced between-study heterogeneity, with residual I² remaining above 89% in all random-effects models. Even exclusion of six extreme outliers reduced I² only modestly (from 98.6% to 98.2%), and the leave-one-out analysis showed I² consistently exceeding 98% regardless of which study was removed. This persistence of high heterogeneity across sensitivity analyses reinforces the meta-regression finding that study-level moderators explain only a fraction of between-study variability, and that unmeasured patient-level and methodological factors are likely responsible for most observed heterogeneity.

### Summary and Interpretation

Collectively, these sensitivity analyses demonstrate that the positive association between shorter TTP and increased mortality is robust to: (1) exclusion of potential outliers; (2) removal of any single study; (3) restriction to higher-quality studies; and (4) restriction to raw 2×2 table data. The pooled odds ratio remained statistically significant and exceeded 1.8 across all random-effects sensitivity analyses, with most estimates clustering between 2.0 and 2.4. The fixed-effect model's substantially lower estimate (OR = 1.04) highlights the importance of appropriate heterogeneity modeling but does not undermine the validity of the random-effects conclusion, as the assumption of a common effect size is clearly violated.

The cumulative meta-analysis suggests possible small-study effects or temporal trends, with earlier studies reporting larger effects. However, the association has remained consistently positive and statistically significant as the evidence base has matured, supporting the conclusion that TTP is a genuine prognostic marker rather than an artifact of early, potentially biased research.

The persistence of high heterogeneity across sensitivity analyses underscores the need for future research to measure and account for key patient-level confounders (disease severity, antibiotic timing, source control) and to employ standardized TTP measurement protocols to enhance comparability across studies.

### Sensitivity Analyses (~250 words)

Comprehensive sensitivity analyses assessed robustness of the TTP-mortality association. Excluding six potential outliers with OR > 6 reduced the pooled estimate to 1.82 (95% CI: 1.14–1.86, k = 27), but the association remained significant. Leave-one-out analysis demonstrated stability, with pooled OR ranging narrowly from 2.15 to 2.39 across all iterations, indicating no single study disproportionately influenced findings.

Restricting to low/moderate risk of bias studies (k = 31) yielded OR 2.12 (95% CI: 1.24–2.74), nearly identical to the main analysis, suggesting robustness to study quality. Analysis limited to 2×2 table data (k = 20) produced OR 2.40 (95% CI: 1.34–6.42) with reduced heterogeneity (I² = 89.1%), suggesting some variability attributable to statistical adjustment differences.

Cumulative meta-analysis by publication year showed early studies reported larger effects (2006: OR = 3.71), with estimates stabilizing around 2.1–2.4 since 2015. However, the association remained consistently positive across all time periods.

A fixed-effect model produced OR 1.04 (95% CI: 1.03–1.06), substantially lower than random-effects estimates, but this reflects inappropriate modeling assumptions given extreme heterogeneity (I² = 98.6%) rather than questioning the validity of findings.

Across all random-effects sensitivity analyses, pooled odds ratios remained above 1.8 and statistically significant, with most estimates between 2.0–2.4, demonstrating robust evidence for the TTP-mortality association independent of analytical choices, outliers, or study quality.
